§15–19

Analysis, transfers & optimizer

This stage takes the projections and the weekly lineup and turns them into squad advice: which slot is weak, which transfer helps, which 15 players to pick, and what a sale really pays. All of it is estimates; only prices and bank are facts.

§15Squad analysis metrics

In short: the app grades each squad slot against same-position peers and rates your lineup against the best lineup money could buy.

These helpers sit in lib/analysis/ and build on the projections. estimate

§15.2Fixture difficulty (normalized)

In short: average the next fixtures' difficulty (3 if unknown) and squeeze it onto a 0 to 1 scale.

avg = mean(fixture.difficulty ?? 3) over the first horizon fixtures fixtureDifficulty = clamp((avg - 1) / 4, 0, 1) // empty schedule → 0.5

So an average of 1 gives 0, an average of 3 gives 0.5, and an average of 5 gives 1.

§15.3Weakness score

In short: six checks, weighted so the weights add to 100; a higher total means a weaker player.

The weights below make one bar of 100 points. Projection and value carry half the score.

The player is compared with same-position peers. percentileDeficit is one minus the share of peers strictly below them, so ties count against the player:

percentileDeficit = 1 - (#peers strictly below) / (peers.length - 1) score = round(clamp(Σ component * weight, 0, 100))

Made-up case: a midfielder sits in a list of 11 midfielders, so peers.length - 1 = 10. If 8 are strictly below this player, the deficit is 1 - 8/10 = 0.2, and the projection check adds 0.2 * 30 = 6. A player below every peer has deficit 1 and adds the full 30.

Detail

The clamp keeps the total between 0 and 100 and the result rounds to a whole number.

Reads offThe two sides price the armband differently. The ceiling adds its captain's full points. The lineup adds captainBonus (§14.7), cut by the chance that the captain and vice-captain do not play. So the rating reads slightly low when the captain is in doubt. §16Transfer suggestions

In short: the app tries swaps, keeps those that gain points and that no other swap beats on both points and cash, and shows the best five.

§16.1Replacements

In short: for one outgoing player, rank same-position candidates by points gained, with small bonuses for minutes (SAFE only) and cash saved.

score = projectedDelta + (SAFE ? expectedMinutes / 100 : 0) + bankDelta / 100 projectedDelta = candidate.nextN - outgoing.nextN bankDelta = outgoing.price - candidate.price

A candidate must pass the club limit, the budget, the exclusions and, for SAFE, the availability and confidence gates.

lib/analysis/replacements.ts · findReplacements

§16.2Single transfers

In short: test every legal one-player swap that gains xP, then drop any swap that another swap beats on both xP and cash.

The chart is an illustration. Each dot is a swap. Gold dots stay; grey dots lose to a gold dot that has more cash and more xP.

cashReleasedTenths = sellingPrice(outgoing) - incoming.priceTenths score = projectedDeltaPerGW + 0.25 * (cashReleasedTenths / 10) // CASH_XP_PER_MILLION

Made-up swap: +0.8 xP per gameweek, and it releases 5 tenths (£0.5m). Score = 0.8 + 0.25 * (5 / 10) = 0.925.

The label is XP_UPGRADE, or BOTH when the swap also releases cash. The route returns the top five.

What projectedDelta counts, and the budget rule

projectedDelta is the change in the §14 weekly lineup total summed over the horizon. It counts bench order, captaincy and autosubs, not just the two players.

The selling price follows the official rule (§19.1) when purchase prices are supplied, and equals the market price otherwise. With a real bank the app does not check the owned squad against the budget, since team value is selling value plus bank. Each swap must instead fit within bank + sellingPrice(outgoing).

§16.4Multi-transfer plans

In short: when a request sets maxTransfers (1 to 5), an exact HiGHS solve replaces §16.2 and finds whole plans of up to that many transfers.

1. Candidatestop 25 per position, plus every owned player
→
2. Solvefor each k, keep exactly 15 - k owned players
→
3. Cut and repeatso the next plan differs
→
4. Rescorefull §14 lineup total over the horizon
→
5. Rankbest five
  • Candidates: incoming players need status a or d, must not be excluded, and under SAFE must not carry availability risk.
  • Variables and constraints: the same squad, weekly XI and captain variables as §17.3: 15 players, 2/5/5/3, at most 3 per club, locked players kept, and for each k exactly 15 - k owned players retained.
  • Budget: Σ cost ≤ bank + Σ sellingPrice(owned). An owned player costs their selling price; an incoming player costs their market price. bank defaults to 0.
  • Objective: each gameweek's starter and captain xP, plus a flat reserve value of 0.15 × xP for an outfield reserve and 0.05 × for a goalkeeper. This is simpler than the pDNP-based reserve weights in §17.3.
reserve value = 0.15 * xP // outfield reserve reserve value = 0.05 * xP // goalkeeper reserve
Plan count, rescoring and ranking

For each k from 1 to maxTransfers, it finds up to max(2, min(3, 5 - k)) distinct plans (five when maxTransfers = 1), adding a cut after each so the next solve must differ. It then rescores every plan with the full §14 weekly lineup total over the horizon, and drops any plan with no xP gain. It pairs outgoing and incoming players by position and orders them so the moves that release the most cash come first.

It returns the five best plans, ranked by: xP gain, then fewer transfers, then more cash released, then lower incoming risk.

§17.3Exact optimizer (HiGHS MILP)

In short: a solver picks 15 players, 11 starters each week and one captain each week to maximize total projected points, under the squad rules.

exactOptimizeFullSquad and exactCompletePartialSquad build a Mixed Integer Linear Program (MILP) and solve it with HiGHS WebAssembly. The squad is planned for planGameweek across horizon (1, 3, 5 or 10 gameweeks) and maximizes raw projected points from gameweekValue(player, gameweek). Chip planning can instead pass an explicit list of gameweeks, such as one gameweek for a Free Hit (§19.3).

The three choices

x: choose 15

x[i] is 0 or 1: is player i in the squad?

s: choose 11 each week

s[g,i] is 0 or 1: does player i start in gameweek g?

c: choose a captain each week

c[g,i] is 0 or 1, for each g from planGameweek to planGameweek + horizon - 1.

What it maximizes

  • Normal solve: Σ xP[g,i] * (s[g,i] + c[g,i]) over all g and i plus a fixed reserve weight. A starter earns xP, and a captain earns it a second time.
  • Bench Boost solve: Σ xP[g,i] * (x[i] + c[g,i]) over all g and i. All 15 squad players count, and the bench strategy is ignored.

Rules it must obey

  • s ≤ x s[g,i] ≤ x[i]: only squad players can start.
  • 15 and 11 Σ x[i] = 15 and Σ s[g,i] = 11 for every gameweek.
  • Budget Σ cost[i] * x[i] ≤ budget. Without finance inputs, cost is the market price and the budget is budgetTenths. When both bankTenths and purchasePricesTenths are supplied, financeContext prices each owned player at their selling price (§19.1) and each incoming player at market price, and sets the budget to bank plus the owned squad's selling value. The app then does not check the owned squad's own market cost against budgetTenths.
  • Positions 2 GK, 5 DEF, 5 MID, 3 FWD in the squad. The starting XI needs 1 GK, at least 3 DEF, at least 2 MID and at least 1 FWD.
  • Clubs Σ x[i] over one club ≤ 3 (or the configured maximum).
  • Locks x[i] = 1 for every fixed or locked player ID.
  • Captain c[g,i] ≤ s[g,i] (the captain starts that week) and Σ c[g,i] = 1 for each gameweek.

Bench value in a normal solve

Reserve coefficients come from the same P(DNP) (chance of not playing) that the weekly autosub model uses. For each gameweek, the optimizer forms a representative legal formation from the highest-xP players. Its ten outfield players have DNP probabilities p1 … p10.

ModeWeight on each outfield reserveIn plain words
BALANCEDmin(1, Σ p / 3)Expected absences, spread over the three reserve slots.
STRONG1 - Π(1 - p)Chance that at least one outfield starter misses the week, so every reserve counts as first cover.
CHEAPthe BALANCED weight, times the position's minimum price ÷ the player's priceCheap reserves keep their value for their price.
  • The backup goalkeeper uses the representative starting goalkeeper's DNP probability in every mode.
  • A reserve's coefficient also includes their own appearance probability, 1 - P(DNP).
  • Under CHEAP, a tiny price tie-break picks the cheaper reserve only when projected values are otherwise equal.
  • The solver precomputes the coefficients, so all three modes use the same variables and one solver pass.
ExactThe result is the true optimum of this model, not a guess.

§19Selling prices, transfers and chips

In short: the money and chip rules, shared by the planner, the optimizer, transfer search and chip suggestions, live in lib/chips/.

§19.1Selling price

In short: you keep half of any price rise, rounded down to the tenth, and you lose all of any fall.

fact prices and purchase prices   (the rule is official).
sellingPrice = current <= purchase ? current : purchase + floor((current - purchase) * 0.5) // sellProfitFraction

Bought at 60 tenths (£6.0m), now 65 (£6.5m). Rise = 5. Half of 5 is 2.5, rounded down to 2. Selling price = 60 + 2 = 62, so £6.2m.

§19.2Transfer accounting

In short: each normal week you get one new free transfer, you can bank up to five, and every transfer past the free balance costs 4 points.

transfers = min(transferCount, 20) // maxTransfersPerGameweek freeUsed = min(transfers, min(freeBefore, 5)) // maxFreeTransfers paid = transfers - freeUsed hitCost = paid * 4 // hitCostPerTransferPoints freeAfter = min(5, freeBefore - freeUsed + 1)

Chip suggestions

/api/chip-suggestions tries each legal gameweek in the planning window and returns the best week for each chip, ranked by incrementalXp.

Bench Boost and Triple Captain

The §14.7 chip total minus the saved lineup's total for that week.

Free Hit

An exact §17.3 solve for that one gameweek, with a budget of bank plus the permanent squad's selling value. It is scored with the §14 lineup engine and compared with the saved plan's net total for that week.
WildcardNever suggested. Its timing turns on fixture swings and news, and a deterministic model always answers "play it now".
