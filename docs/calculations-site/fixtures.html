§7

Fixture adjustment

A player scores more against a weak side at home. This stage turns one fixture into three numbers: how much it lifts or cuts attack, how likely a clean sheet is, and how many goals the team should concede.

§7Fixture adjustment

In short: each fixture yields exactly three numbers, and every later points calculation reads them.

§7.1Base difficulty and venue

In short: an easy fixture lifts attack by up to 14%, a hard one cuts it by up to 16%, and playing at home adds about 10% on top.

The five anchor values sit at difficulty 1 to 5, and continuousDifficultyMultiplier joins them with straight lines. Difficulty 2.5, for example, falls halfway between 1.07 and 1.00.

difficulty = fixture.exactDifficulty ?? fixture.difficulty ?? 3 // Continuous ClubElo FDR from §2.2 base = continuousDifficultyMultiplier(difficulty) // Linear interpolation over [1.14, 1.07, 1.00, 0.92, 0.84] venue = home ? 1.102 : 0.898 attackMultiplier = base * venue expectedGoalsAgainst = 1.35 * (home ? 0.9 : 1.1) // fallback only - see §7.3

The home edge, measured evidence

Over all 380 fixtures of 2025/26, home sides averaged 1.551 xG and away sides 1.264. Against the 1.408 league mean, that gives 1.102 and 0.898.

Evidence and caveats
  • Actual goals agree: 1.453 at home against 1.203 away.
  • The earlier pair 1.03 / 0.97 held about a third of the real spread. It also contradicted the 0.9 / 1.1 on the goals-against side of the same fixture.
  • Re-derive the pair with npx tsx scripts/backtest/sweep.ts.
  • base now reflects the ClubElo scale, not a live FPL label. The neutral fallback stays 3, so a club with no mapping keeps base = 1.00.
  • The older walk-forward test in scripts/backtest/fdr.ts measured FPL's own FDR, so it does not validate this replacement.
  • ClubElo difficulty and the strength ratio (§7.2) both describe the matchup, and the model still multiplies them. That weighting is an explicit model assumption.
  • Real historical ClubElo is now available (§2.2.1), but the earlier FDR studies keep their original proxy or FPL inputs.

§7.2Strength-based adjustment

In short: when both teams have strengths, the model also multiplies by how well this attack matches that defence, held between 0.70 and 1.35.

Two clamps work in turn. The ratio clamp does the real work. The outer clamp is wider on purpose, so it catches only an absurd input.

attackMultiplier *= clamp(ownAttack / opponentDefence, 0.70, 1.35) attackMultiplier = clamp(attackMultiplier, 0.55, 1.60) // Without strengths: expectedGoalsAgainst is divided by base^2.

cleanSheetProbability no longer comes from this ratio. See §7.4.

lib/projections/fixtureAdjustment.ts

Why 0.70 to 1.35 evidence
  • The old ratio window was 0.78-1.22. Walk-forward strengths for 2025/26 gave ratios from 0.47 to 1.62, so the old clamp bit on 24.5% of team-fixtures and could not tell a good matchup from a great one.
  • Widening it to 0.70-1.35 cut expected-points RMSE by 0.0008. The paired confidence interval excludes zero, and the change won 24 of 33 gameweeks.
  • The gain stays flat from there to unclamped, so this is the narrowest window that keeps all of it. A window is still needed: blendInSeasonForm floors a defence ratio at 0.05, which would let an unclamped ratio reach about 25.
Why the outer clamp is 0.55 to 1.60: the Haaland case

It used to be 0.7-1.3, which bound on 14% of forward-fixtures. Over 2025/26 it flattened Erling Haaland's 30 fixtures onto a 0.89-1.30 range when the inputs said 0.89-1.57. 11 of the 30 sat pinned on the ceiling, so the model could not tell his best fixture from his median one. Forwards' modelled swing between their easiest and hardest fixtures came to 0.73 points against an observed 1.07.

Calibration fix, not an accuracy win

Widening this clamp left match-level RMSE unchanged (+0.0006, interval spanning zero) and top-30 selection flat. Single-match points are too noisy to reward the right slope. The case for the change is that a ceiling that erases a third of a player's fixture variation misleads anyone who plans a fixture run.

What happens without either clamp evidence

Removing either clamp is worse, and the margin grows with the spread of the level. Over 660 walk-forward team-fixtures, opening both windows costs +0.0121 team-xG RMSE, with the paired interval excluding zero. On top of a widened level (§3.4) it costs +0.1283. The two shipped windows together bite on 16.4% of team-fixtures.

§7.3Consistent goals-against

In short: the model turns the clean-sheet probability back into goals against, so both numbers tell the same story.

P(no goals)= exp(−xGA) for a Poisson count
⇄
xGA= −ln(p)

This inversion runs after a table lookup (§7.4.1) and when no lookup happened. On the Elo path (§7.4), expectedGoalsAgainst is the fitted mean instead. If no table lookup happened, the clean-sheet probability is clamp(exp(-expectedGoalsAgainst), 0.03, 0.65). The model then re-derives the goals-against number from the clean-sheet probability:

expectedGoalsAgainst = -ln(clamp(cleanSheetProbability, 0.03, 0.9))

This is the single Poisson parameter that the goals-conceded deduction (§8) uses.

Made-up probability: cleanSheetProbability = 0.30. Then expectedGoalsAgainst = -ln(0.30) = 1.204. The fallback in §7.1 gives 1.35 * 0.9 = 1.215 at home and 1.35 * 1.1 = 1.485 away, before this step.

Where this applies

The spec words this as the case where no table lookup happened. On the Elo path (§7.4), expectedGoalsAgainst is the fitted mean and not this inversion.

§7.4Clean sheets from Elo and goal lean

In short: the model rates each team by Elo, shifts toward this season's xG as matches pile up, and reads the chance of zero goals off a negative binomial curve.

The table in §7.4.1 stays as the fallback for any fixture where either side has no ClubElo rating.

1. Splitlevel = log(attack) + log(defence)
skew = log(attack) − log(defence)
→
2. LevelElo level, blended with the fitted level by matches played
→
3. Skewshrunk to 0.6 of its size
→
4. Goals against1.408 × attack′opponent / defence′own × venue
→
5. P(0 goals)negative binomial, phi 12

deriveCleanSheetStrengths splits each team's §3 strengths into two parts:

level_i = log(attack_i) + log(defence_i) skew_i = log(attack_i) - log(defence_i)

The level says how good the team is overall. The skew says whether it leans to attack or defence.

Level and skew

eloLevel_i = ELO_LEVEL_SLOPE * (elo_i - meanElo) // ELO_LEVEL_SLOPE = 0.0034 fitLevel_i = log(attack_i) + log(defence_i) stretch = sd(eloLevel) / sd(fitLevel) // 0 when sd(fitLevel) <= 1e-9 fitWeight_i = matchesPlayed_i / (matchesPlayed_i + 12) level'_i = (1 - fitWeight_i) * eloLevel_i + fitWeight_i * (fitLevel_i - meanFitLevel) * stretch skew'_i = 0.6 * (log(attack_i) - log(defence_i)) attack'_i = exp((level'_i + skew'_i) / 2) defence'_i = exp((level'_i - skew'_i) / 2)

The means and standard deviations use the rated teams in the call. With no matches-played map, every fit weight is zero. CLEAN_SHEET_LEVEL_PRIOR_MATCHES is 12, so after 12 played matches Elo and the fitted level weigh the same.

0 matchesfit 0.00
6 matchesfit 0.33
12 matchesfit 0.50
38 matchesfit 0.76

Bars show fitWeight = matches / (matches + 12), the share of the level that comes from this season's xG fit. The 6, 12 and 38 match rows apply the spec formula to those counts.

The calculation takes an explicit Map<teamId, elo>. It does not resolve club names or read a snapshot. Normalization supplies ClubElo ratings in production, and backtests can supply dated ratings through the same interface. A missing or non-finite rating leaves that club out of the rated path. Production keeps ELO_LEVEL_SLOPE = 0.0034; a backtest can pass another slope.

From strengths to a probability

The probability is P(0 goals) under a negative binomial:

goalsAgainst = LEAGUE_MEAN_XG * (attack'_opponent / defence'_own) * venue venue = 0.898 at home, 1.102 away LEAGUE_MEAN_XG = 1.408 cleanSheetProbability = clamp((phi / (phi + goalsAgainst))^phi, 0.02, 0.9) phi = 12

lib/projections/cleanSheetStrength.ts · deriveCleanSheetStrengths

The curve is the formula above, drawn for goalsAgainst from 0 to 3.5. The dashed line is the Poisson curve, which sits lower for the typical range of goals against.

Take two evenly matched teams (attack′ / defence′ = 1, a made-up case).

  • Home: goalsAgainst = 1.408 * 1 * 0.898 = 1.264. Then (12 / 13.264)^12 = 0.3006. The Poisson value is exp(−1.264) = 0.282.
  • Away: goalsAgainst = 1.408 * 1 * 1.102 = 1.552. Then (12 / 13.552)^12 = 0.2324.

What changes downstream

  • No compression. CLEAN_SHEET_RETAINED_WEIGHT corrects a top-end error that the table makes and this read does not. It applies only on the table path (§7.4.1).
  • expectedGoalsAgainst is the fitted mean, not the Poisson inversion of the probability. Inverting a negative binomial as if it were a Poisson understates the mean by about 5% at a typical goals-against.
Why Elo supplies the level evidence

Elo measures a team's overall level with years of memory, not a few gameweeks of xG. It rates promoted clubs on the same scale as everyone else: Coventry, Hull and Ipswich have no top-flight goal history at all. It cannot tell an attacking side from a defensive one at the same rating. The xG fit does that well, so the model keeps the lean from §3.

Why ELO_LEVEL_SLOPE is fixed, not refitted evidence

An independent Poisson fitted to all 380 fixtures of 2025/26 xG gives 0.00340 log units of level per Elo point, r = 0.949. The §3 strengths are normalized ratios whose level spread is 1.69x narrower than that fit (sd 0.189 against 0.319). Regressing against them would copy the narrowness, so Elo would re-order teams that it should also spread apart.

Why phi is 12 evidence

The model sweeps phi on realized clean sheets over 760 team-fixtures. Brier is flat from 10 to 15 (0.18569) and worse on either side. The Poisson limit is the worst of the sweep at 0.18601.

A Poisson understates clean sheets because goals arrive in lumps, more than the xG that the rates are fitted on suggests. It puts the 2025/26 league rate at 0.249 against an actual 0.270.

Market comparison evidence

Against the gameweek-4 2026/27 bookmaker clean-sheet market, with no parameter fitted to that market, this reads MAE 2.97 against the table's 5.67. The spread is sd 9.23, against the market's 10.27 and the table's 7.25.

How much of that is ClubElo, and how much is the scale. Mostly the scale. The next table holds everything else fixed and varies only the level term, against the same market:

Scope of the earlier evidence
  • The walk-forward arm in scripts/backtest/cleansheets.ts could not resolve a gain against realized clean sheets, and neither could team-level.ts. Taking the level from ClubElo scores Brier 0.18287 against 0.18237 for the strengths' own level. Both sit inside a corpus that resolves nothing below +/-0.0025. These tests did not resolve an incremental gain from the Elo term.
  • Those arms read the synthetic ratings in elo-history.ts, not ClubElo's. Real dated ClubElo values now come from the embedded charts on public club pages (§2.2.1). The earlier results still measure their original proxy input and have not been rerun on the new archive.
  • The gameweek-4 market table has 20 values, and matching it is not evidence about outcomes. Twice, a change that improved market agreement did worse against realized results: the grid extrapolation arm in the backtest README, and the level rescale in §3.4. The market's clean-sheet odds look like a wide surface derived from ratings, so a ratings-driven model agrees with them partly by sharing their construction.

The earlier negative-binomial versus table comparison was unresolved (Brier 0.1824 versus 0.1847). The later Tier C held-out distribution test also supports the current mix. Switching clean sheets to Poisson raised pooled whole-xP RMSE by 0.00153 [95% gameweek-cluster interval 0.00058, 0.00247]. Using negative binomial for both clean sheets and conceded deductions did not resolve an xP gain and worsened conceded-count likelihood. Those tests used proxy Elo and observed player minutes. See scripts/backtest/results/tier-c-conceded.md. The live ClubElo source and both distribution choices stay unchanged.

Tiers are anchored at consensusStrengthTiers = [0.84, 0.92, 1.00, 1.08, 1.16] with TIER_STEP = 0.08.

interpolatedCleanSheet does not snap a continuous strength onto a whole tier. It reads the grid with a 2D bilinear interpolation: it finds a point between four neighbouring cells and weights each by how close the point lies.

r = clamp((ownDefence - 0.84) / 0.08, 0, 4) c = clamp((opponentAttack - 0.84) / 0.08, 0, 4) // then clamp the result to 0.02 - 0.9

This gives smooth changes in probability as team form shifts through the season. It removes artificial jumps and keeps the exact values on whole tier coordinates.

Made-up team: home, ownDefence = 0.88, opponentAttack = 1.00.

  1. r = (0.88 − 0.84) / 0.08 = 0.5 and c = (1.00 − 0.84) / 0.08 = 2.
  2. c = 2 is column tier 3 exactly. r = 0.5 lies halfway between tier 1 and tier 2 rows.
  3. Home table: tier 1 gives 0.20 and tier 2 gives 0.27, so the result is (0.20 + 0.27) / 2 = 0.235.

Compression at the top

Reads above the long-run clean-sheet rate (0.25) are compressed one-sided toward it. They keep 75% of the excess (CLEAN_SHEET_RETAINED_WEIGHT). The bottom is left alone.

Made-up read

A table read of 0.50 sits 0.25 above 0.25. It keeps 75% of that, 0.1875, so it becomes 0.25 + 0.1875 = 0.4375. A read of 0.20 stays 0.20.

CLEAN_SHEET_RETAINED_WEIGHT · lib/projections/fixtureAdjustment.ts

Backtest evidence evidence
  • Over 660 walk-forward team-fixtures, continuous bilinear interpolation lowers clean-sheet Brier to 0.18469 and logloss to 0.55321 (against 0.18527 and 0.55452 for discrete snapping).
  • With schedule-adjusted team form (§3.3), it lowered walk-forward xP RMSE to 2.69866 (-0.00162 against the discrete baseline), winning 19 of 33 gameweeks.
  • The table's top quintile over-projected defender clean-sheet-plus-conceded points by +0.20 per appearance over 2023/24-2024/25. The bottom two quintiles calibrated, so the bottom stays as it is.
  • Weights 0.70-0.90 all beat the unshrunk table on team-level Brier in both seasons. Full-xP dRMSE is -0.00163, with the paired gameweek-cluster interval excluding zero.
